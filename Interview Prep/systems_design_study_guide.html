Study Guide [Systems Design]

From zero to L5-ready in ~2 hours/day. Full-stack weighting: deep on APIs, data modeling, caching, and real-time; conversational on infra internals (know what Kafka does and when, not how it stores segments). Ratio: weeks 1–2 build vocabulary, weeks 3–4 are practice — the interview skill is doing designs, not reading about them.

Priority tiers — where your hours go

COLD
Must know cold: request lifecycle, SQL vs NoSQL + indexes, caching (all layers + invalidation), replication vs sharding, load balancing, queues & async work, pagination, WebSocket/SSE/polling, idempotency, back-of-envelope math, CAP in plain words.
TALK
Know well enough to discuss: consistent hashing, rate limiting algorithms, CDC/outbox, search indexing (Elasticsearch), blob storage + CDN, fan-out strategies, distributed locks, auth (sessions/JWT/OAuth).
SKIP
Skip for full-stack L5: consensus internals (Raft/Paxos), Kafka/DB storage internals, Kubernetes, service mesh, gossip protocols, custom infra design (design a database, design Kafka).

Week 1 — Foundations

how one server becomes many
Goal: draw and defend the default skeleton — client → CDN → LB → stateless app tier → cache → DB — and explain every arrow.

Day 1 · Request lifecycle

DNS → LB → app server → DB and back. Vertical vs horizontal scaling; why stateless app servers scale horizontally for free.

Day 2 · Databases I

SQL vs NoSQL — the honest answer: Postgres until proven otherwise. Indexes: what a B-tree index buys, what writes pay. Model a schema for a small app.

Day 3 · Databases II

Replication (read replicas, primary failover, replication lag) vs sharding/partitioning (choosing a partition key, hot partitions). When each is the answer.

Day 4 · Caching

The full ladder: browser → CDN → app cache (Redis) → DB. TTL vs explicit invalidation ("the two hard things"), cache-aside pattern, eviction (LRU), stampede.

Day 5 · Consistency, plainly

CAP without the theory: on a network split, do you serve stale (available) or refuse (consistent)? Strong vs eventual with product examples — bank balance vs like count.

Days 6–7 · Consolidate

Do the math drill (panel below) until fast. Design "URL shortener" cold — the hello-world of system design. Note what you couldn't answer; that's Monday's reading.

Week 2 — Communication & async

your full-stack strength zone
Goal: own the client-to-server contract better than backend-only candidates — this is where you differentiate.

Day 8 · API design

REST conventions, error shapes, versioning. GraphQL vs REST vs gRPC in one paragraph each. Pagination: offset vs cursor and why offset dies at depth.

Day 9 · Real-time

Polling vs long-polling vs SSE vs WebSocket — cost, direction, reconnect story, when each wins. The full-stack view: what the client does on disconnect.

Day 10 · Queues & async

Message queue vs pub-sub; SQS vs Kafka positioning (task queue vs replayable log). Retries, dead-letter queues, and why retries force idempotency.

Day 11 · Idempotency & reliability

Idempotency keys on writes, exactly-once is a lie (at-least-once + dedupe), webhooks with signed payloads + retry backoff. Plaid-country material.

Day 12 · Edges of the system

CDN + blob storage (S3) for media, presigned upload URLs. Rate limiting (token bucket in one diagram). Auth in five sentences: sessions vs JWT, OAuth flow shape.

Days 13–14 · Consolidate

Design "typeahead/autocomplete" and "notification system" cold, using the playbook, 45 min each, out loud. Grade yourself against the playbook's scoring panel.

Week 3 — Practice designs

one per day, 45 min, out loud, playbook open
Goal: eight designs done. Each one exists to teach a specific concept — after finishing, read a reference solution and note what you missed.
  • Day 15 · News feed (Twitter/Instagram) — teaches fan-out on write vs read, celebrity problem. The single most-asked question; do it twice.
  • Day 16 · Chat (WhatsApp/Slack) — WebSockets at scale, message ordering, delivery receipts, online presence.
  • Day 17 · Ticket/booking (Ticketmaster) — contention: two users, one seat. Locking vs atomic conditional writes; reservations with TTL.
  • Day 18 · Live dashboard (metrics/fleet monitoring) — ingest pipeline, time-series aggregation, push vs pull to the client. Waymo-flavored.
  • Day 19 · Payment/webhook system — idempotency end to end, retries, reconciliation, audit log. Plaid-flavored.
  • Day 20 · Rate limiter + URL shortener redux — both small; practice doing a tight 25-minute version. Teaches pacing.
  • Day 21 · Collaborative doc or multi-tenant app builder — permissions, tenant isolation, optimistic UI + conflict handling. Retool-flavored.

Week 4 — Mocks & sharpening

simulate, patch, rest
Goal: three full mocks under real conditions. By now the playbook sequence should run on rails; mocks expose where it derails.

Days 22–26 · Mock cycle ×3

Mock (45 min, unseen question, spoken aloud or with a partner/AI interviewer) → same-day review: which step ran long, which deep dive was thin → one day patching that gap → next mock.

Day 27 · Company pass

One hour per upcoming interview: reread their product, pick the 2 most likely question shapes (panel below), sketch each for 15 min. Not full designs — just openings.

Day 28 · Rest

Skim the playbook and the math drill. No new material. Cramming week-4 infra trivia costs more than it buys.

Back-of-envelope kit — memorize

  • QPS: requests/day ÷ 100,000 ≈ avg QPS. Peak ≈ 3–5×. (1M/day ≈ 12 QPS — tiny.)
  • One Postgres box comfortably does thousands of simple QPS — don't shard early.
  • Storage: 1M rows × 1 KB = 1 GB. A "huge" 1 TB table still fits one machine.
  • Latency ladder: memory ~100ns · SSD ~100µs · same-region network ~1ms · cross-region ~50–150ms · disk seek ~10ms. Conclusion that matters: cache hits are ~100× cheaper than DB reads, cross-region is the killer.
  • Sizes: UUID 16 B · timestamp 8 B · tweet ~280 B · image ~500 KB · 1 min video ~50 MB (→ blob storage + CDN, never the DB).

Default technology shortlist

  • Postgres — default store. Redis — cache, counters, locks. Kafka/SQS — async + spikes. S3 + CDN — media. Elasticsearch — text search. DynamoDB/Cassandra — massive simple-key write scale.
  • One reason each, never the internals. "Redis because sub-ms reads for hot data" is complete.

Company flavors — your five targets

  • Waymo: real-time and latency-aware — fleet dashboards, log/metrics ingestion, freshness trade-offs. Lean on Day 18. Their infra prompts (petabyte lidar pipelines) are for infra roles; full-stack gets the dashboard/data-product version.
  • Plaid: reliability of money-adjacent data — webhooks, idempotency, retries, reconciliation, third-party APIs that fail. Day 11 + Day 19 are the prep.
  • Google: most classic — scale numbers, trade-off rigor, crisp requirements→design traceability. The playbook as-is, executed cleanly.
  • Retool: product-minded full-stack — multi-tenancy, permissions/RBAC, query execution against customer DBs, optimistic UI. Day 21.
  • Common denominator: every one rewards the API + data-model step done well — your strongest step. Never rush it.

Resources (in order)
