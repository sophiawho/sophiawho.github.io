Must Review Before Google

#4 Median of Two Sorted Arrays

Binary search on the smaller array to find the partition where left halves ≤ right halves. The trick is searching for a cut point, not a value. Classic Google hard — know the partition invariant and the odd/even length handling cold.

#42 Trapping Rain Water

Water above each bar = min(maxLeft, maxRight) − height. Three ways: prefix-max arrays (O(n) space), two pointers moving inward from the shorter side (O(1) space), or a monotonic stack. Two-pointer is the one to have ready. A Google staple.

Two PointersMonotonic StackHard

#210 Course Schedule II — Topological Sort

Kahn's: build indegree counts, seed a queue with indegree-0 nodes, pop and decrement neighbors, append to order. If the order is shorter than n, there's a cycle. Double-duty with the Scale Task Prioritizer prep — same algorithm, priority-queue variant there.

#131 Palindrome Partitioning

Backtracking: for each start index, try every prefix that's a palindrome, recurse on the rest, undo. Choose / explore / unchoose — the same skeleton as #78/#46. Optionally precompute an isPalindrome[i][j] DP table to skip re-checking.

BacktrackingMedium

#298 Binary Tree Longest Consecutive Sequence

DFS carrying two things down: the parent's value and the current run length. If node.val == parent.val + 1, extend the run, else reset to 1. Track a global max. One clean post/pre-order pass, O(n).

Tree DFSMedium

#302 Smallest Rectangle Enclosing Black Pixels

Given one black pixel, binary search each of the four borders: the columns/rows split cleanly into "has a black pixel" vs "doesn't", so you can binary search for the leftmost, rightmost, top, bottom black boundary. O(m log n + n log m) beats the O(mn) flood fill. The hardest of the set — review the "projection is monotonic" argument.

Binary SearchHard
Behavioural Bank
Everything below maps onto your 5 STAR stories: impact, disagreement, failure, simplifying complexity, ownership beyond scope. Google probes hard, so every story needs two extra layers ready: "can you tell me more about that?" (specific details, numbers, your exact actions) and "what would you have done differently?" (a reflective alternative). 90 seconds spoken, "I" not "we", quantified result.
Challenging-project question — prep this as a full walkthrough, it's the most likely opener:
  1. Project background — one sentence of context
  2. Your specific role — "I", not "we"
  3. What exactly made it challenging — the real constraint, not "it was hard"
  4. Actions you took — concrete decisions and trade-offs
  5. Final result — quantified
  6. What you learned — the reflective close Google wants
Describe a challenging project and what made it challenging.
→ Maps to your simplifying-complexity or impact story. Lead with the specific constraint. Follow the 6-point frame above.
Tell me about a time you disagreed with a team member and how you resolved it.
→ Disagreement story. Probes: tell me more about the specifics · what would you have done differently? Show you heard them out and reached a decision on merits, not seniority.
Describe a situation where you had to deliver under a very tight deadline.
→ Impact or ownership story. Probes: more specifics · what would you do differently? Name what you cut and why the trade-off was right.
Tell me about a project you're most proud of and why.
→ Your strongest impact story. Probes: more specifics · alternatives. Lead with the quantified result, then the "why it mattered".
How do you handle receiving critical feedback?
→ Failure / growth story. Probes: more specifics · what would you do differently? Show a concrete change you made after the feedback, not just "I took it well".
Give an example of when you took ownership of a problem outside your responsibility.
→ Ownership-beyond-scope story. Probes: more specifics · alternatives. This is the Googliness ownership signal — make the "not my job, but I did it anyway" explicit.
What would you do if a colleague's project progress was not keeping up?
→ Hypothetical — no story needed, but answer with a real instinct: understand why first, offer help, escalate only if blocked. Tie to a real time you did this if you have one.
If your manager gave you an extremely difficult task, how would you handle it? Have you faced this?
→ Hypothetical + story. Break it down, clarify scope, find the first tractable piece, communicate early. Anchor to your challenging-project story.
